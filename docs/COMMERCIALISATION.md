# R-MANTRA access and commercialisation

R-MANTRA is designed to be useful without an institution, subscription or `.edu` email address. Commercial services should fund reliable hosting, collaboration and governance without removing ownership of a researcher's data.

## Access principle

- The MIT-licensed self-hosted Community edition remains free.
- The hosted Free tier is open to any valid email domain and should not privilege `.edu` users.
- Users can export a complete workspace backup and a tabular CSV at any time.
- Paid plans add hosted services and administration; they do not make a researcher's own records hostage to the platform.

## Proposed hosted tiers

The limits and prices below are a product hypothesis to validate with researchers, not a currently active charge.

| Tier | Intended user | Proposed inclusion | Indicative positioning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Community / self-hosted | Individuals and technical teams | Unlimited local projects, boards, milestones, local compliance checks, backups and public add-ons | Free and open source |
| Hosted Free | Any researcher, no academic email required | Up to 3 active cloud projects, core boards, local compliance, 2 collaborators per project and portable export | Free |
| Researcher Pro | Active authors | Unlimited active projects, secure multi-device sync, reminders, richer integrations, version history and small-team collaboration | Approximately £8–£12 per month after validation |
| Lab / Research Group | PIs, labs and centres | Shared templates, workload overview, team roles, private add-ons, central billing and administrative controls | Per-user or per-group subscription |
| Institution | Universities, publishers and research organisations | SSO, managed deployment, audit and retention controls, governance review, support agreement and optional private cloud/on-premise hosting | Annual contract |

## Additional revenue routes

1. Premium connectors where maintenance or third-party infrastructure has a real cost.
2. Institution-specific workflow and compliance packs.
3. Managed private deployments, migration and support.
4. An optional add-on marketplace with transparent developer revenue sharing.
5. Training and workflow-design services for research groups.

Licensed similarity services, paid literature databases and other third-party usage should be charged transparently or connected through an institution's existing licence. They should not be disguised as part of a platform fee.

## What should never be monetised

- Selling manuscript content, research topics or collaborator activity.
- Training external AI models on unpublished work without separate, explicit consent.
- Charging users to export their own data.
- Making an automated compliance or AI-detection score a condition of access.
- Ranking individual researchers by publication volume or journal prestige.

## Commercial validation before charging

Interview independent researchers, doctoral researchers, non-`.edu` users, laboratory managers and research-office staff. Validate which hosted capabilities save enough time to justify payment, then publish final limits and pricing before collecting payment.
